The Opportunity

Budget pressures, development challenges, competing priorities, and difficult decisions place demands on City leadership. Meeting those demands depends on individual capability and on how effectively leaders operate together.

The central question

How can these 25–30 leaders work together more effectively?

Build practices that become part of how leaders work throughout the year.

01 / ALIGN

Create a shared language

Launch with an in-person session and observable leadership commitments.

02 / SUSTAIN

Keep the work moving

Use a small Guiding Team to reinforce progress through monthly working conversations.

03 / STRENGTHEN

Learn through real work

Reconvene the broader group quarterly around the City’s current challenges.

02 / Leadership Alignment

Start with understanding.
Leave with commitments.

The initial in-person session brings the full group of 25–30 leaders together to connect individual insight with practical ways of working.

01

Understand ourselves and each other

Introduce a common framework for how leaders naturally contribute, where they may struggle, and how their differences affect the people around them.

02

Understand how we work together

Examine how decisions are made, who needs to be involved, where work stalls, and where the organization relies too heavily on particular people.

03

Establish leadership commitments

Agree on a small number of behaviors that leaders can observe, practice, and reinforce in day-to-day City operations.

Questions for the room

  • Who needs to be involved, and when?
  • What creates unnecessary frustration?
  • Which conversations happening outside the room belong inside it?
  • Where could we make better use of one another’s capabilities?

Proposed session outputs

  • A shared language for contribution and collaboration.
  • A short list of recurring barriers to effective teamwork.
  • Observable leadership commitments.
  • Priority issues for the Guiding Team to carry forward.

From insight to practice

The value of the session is demonstrated in subsequent meetings, decisions, delegation, and cross-department work.

03 / The Guiding Team

Give the work
people who will carry it.

A smaller Leadership Guiding Team keeps the initiative connected to normal City operations as workloads and competing priorities return.

5–8Credible, influential leaders
MonthlyVirtual working conversations
CitywidePerspective and coordination

Selection and purpose

Phil and Veronica would identify leaders with the credibility and organizational influence to carry the work forward. Membership should reflect respect, influence, and the ability to work across departments; it need not consist only of the highest-ranking leaders.

Look for leaders who…

  • Are respected by other leaders.
  • Influence how work gets done.
  • Communicate effectively.
  • See beyond their own department.
  • Will challenge existing habits.
  • Can reinforce new expectations in everyday operations.

The team’s working role

  • Notice progress and recurring barriers.
  • Surface avoided conversations.
  • Identify coordination needs.
  • Reinforce agreed leadership behaviors.
  • Help shape quarterly sessions around real organizational needs.

The full leadership group participates in the change. The smaller team helps lead it.

The initial vision connects this approach with the guiding coalition concept in John Kotter’s change work and the change leadership team concept in Blanchard’s framework.

04 / The Annual Rhythm

A consistent cadence.
Relevant conversations.

Monthly Guiding Team conversations sustain momentum. Quarterly in-person sessions bring the broader leadership group back to the issues that matter most.

Monthly / Leadership Guiding Team / Virtual

Working conversations focused on progress, returning habits, avoided conversations, stalled work, coordination needs, and the people who should be involved in decisions.

Proposed close: Agree on the actions and behaviors to reinforce before the next meeting and carry emerging issues into the next quarterly session.

Q1

Leading ourselves and each other

Understand differences, strengthen communication and trust, and clarify shared expectations.

Q2

Working across departments

Reduce silos, improve coordination, clarify ownership, and move shared work forward.

Q3

Leading through difficult decisions

Address prioritization, budget pressures, accountability, difficult conversations, and communicating decisions.

Q4

Strengthening the leadership system

Review progress, identify persistent barriers, reinforce expectations, and establish priorities for the following year.

Adapt to the City’s needs. Quarterly themes are proposed starting points. Content should remain flexible enough to address the issues San Angelo is facing at the time.

Scheduling detail to confirm: whether the initial alignment session serves as the Q1 session or is an additional engagement.

06 / Results and Next Steps

Make progress visible
in everyday leadership.

Success means leaders can point to noticeable changes in how they communicate, make decisions, and move work forward together.

More direct conversations

Important issues are discussed with the appropriate people. Leadership disagreements are addressed directly.

Clearer decisions and ownership

Leaders understand who needs to participate, when to involve others, and who owns the next step.

Stronger coordination

Cross-department work moves with greater clarity, and leaders make better use of the capabilities around them.

Greater shared capacity

The organization handles difficult periods with more leaders contributing, reducing reliance on a few people to carry everything.

Proposed way to review progress

At launch, identify a few real examples of stalled work, unclear ownership, or avoided conversations. Revisit those examples during monthly and quarterly sessions, noting what changed, what remains difficult, and the next action.
