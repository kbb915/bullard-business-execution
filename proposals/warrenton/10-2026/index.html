Recognize the limiting story. Return to the commitment.

03 / The Leadership Focus

Recognize the story.
Choose the commitment.

The 2–3 hour leadership module will help team members identify limiting stories in themselves and in group conversations, then shift toward commitments that support action.

01 / NOTICE

Hear the story

Recognize language, assumptions, and behaviors that narrow options or preserve the current pattern.

02 / EXAMINE

See the result

Connect the story to the behaviors it produces and the outcomes it keeps creating.

03 / CHOOSE

Live the commitment

Name the commitment, identify different behaviors, and act from what the team wants to create.

What success sounds like

“Pause. We’re in a limiting story. What are we committed to here?”

A shared language the team can use in meetings, decisions, and difficult conversations.

Individual awareness

  • Catch limiting stories earlier.
  • Recognize default behaviors.
  • Choose a more useful response.

Team awareness

  • Name the pattern without blame.
  • Return discussion to shared commitments.
  • Make more productive choices together.

04 / Practice With Real Work

Turn the Town’s work
into the practice field.

The session will use current scenarios supplied by the team so leaders practice the model where it matters—not in abstract case studies.

01

Bring the real scenario

Leaders identify a current budget, service, people, or organizational issue where the conversation is stuck or the default response is repeating itself.

02

Expose the limiting story

The team identifies the assumptions being treated as facts, the behaviors those assumptions produce, and how the pattern constrains action.

03

Clarify the commitment

Leaders name what they are committed to creating and identify the behaviors, conversations, and decisions that commitment requires.

04

Carry it into the agenda

Kevin coaches the team as it applies the language during the remainder of the offsite’s budget, planning, and organizational discussions.

Design principle

The leadership work succeeds when the team can use it Monday morning. Every exercise will connect the model to a live Warrenton issue.

05 / Making It Stick

Carry the language
beyond the offsite.

A single day can create a shared language. Sustained change requires leaders who reinforce it after normal workloads and habits return.

WeeklySLT meetings where the language can be used
3–4Potential internal champions
QuarterlyFuture offsite rhythm envisioned by Jud

Town Manager reinforcement

  • Use the language consistently.
  • Pause limiting-story conversations.
  • Reconnect the team to commitments.
  • Model the shift in real time.

Internal champions

  • Identify influential leaders who understand the model.
  • Help carry the language across the SLT.
  • Reinforce commitments between offsites.
  • Support future organizational change.

The goal is development that changes how the team works—not training that gets placed on a shelf.

Optional continuation

A separate proposal can outline coaching for Jud and two to four key leaders, support for the internal champion group, and facilitation at future quarterly offsites. A Working Genius refresh can be considered for a later session.
